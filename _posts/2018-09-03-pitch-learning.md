[Here](../../papers/iterated_pitch_learning.pdf) is a manuscript describing a small experiment I conducted last summer that tries to address the following question:

> *Which pitch-related aspects of music can be explained by memory biases?*

The experiment resembles the children's game *Telephone* in which a phrase
is whispered from person to person and is transformed in funny ways as people
mishear it. Here, musicians memorize and transcribe sequences of pitches, their
transcription becoming the sequence the next participant is asked to memorize.

<img src="{{ site.url }}/images/transmission_chain.jpg" width="800" align="middle" alt="transmission chain">

The idea of this game (besides having fun, of course) is to model the process of
cultural transmission. If the memory biases of participants work to make the pitch
sequences more closely resemble world music, then this raises the possibility
that many features of world music can be explained by these memory biases compounded
by the process of cultural transmission (although
it's possible that the causality runs the other way --- more on this in the manuscript).

Anyways, the experiment, although ultimately inconclusive, should also be interesting and provocative to some small group of people. If you happen to be one of these
people, send me an email!
